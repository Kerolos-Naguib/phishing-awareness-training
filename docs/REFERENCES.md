## Corrections made during review

Checking the sources showed that earlier drafts contained wording the sources do not support. These were fixed in both deliverables:

1. The CISA 84% figure was reworded to CISA's own wording; an added interpretation was removed.
2. The IC3 totals are labelled as covering all internet crime types.
3. The DOJ says the company had the *same* name as the real supplier, not a similar one.
4. Victim names are attributed to CyberScoop and a Lithuanian court order, not to the DOJ.
5. "Notify your bank" is general guidance and is no longer attributed to the FBI.
6. The FTC article, the CyberScoop article, the FBI article and NIST's own Phish Scale document were added to the reference lists.
7. The Phish Scale slide is labelled as a simplified version of NIST's scale.

## Teaching points that are general practice (no agency source)

Requiring a second approver for large payments; reading a web address right to left to find the real domain; the `@` trick in addresses; the double extension in file names (`.pdf.exe`); "enable content" prompts in documents; never reading a verification code aloud; hanging up and calling back on a known number. Adapt them to your organization's policy.
